From policy to an inspectable result

A small experiment you can explain.

  1. Define the boundary.A validated synthetic fixture states who can access which matters and documents.
  2. Observe the application.Record the document IDs exposed to its answering step, alongside its response.
  3. Evaluate the evidence.Forbidden IDs or exact forbidden canaries produce FAIL. Incomplete evidence without a proven violation produces ERROR, not PASS.

Scope matters

What this does—and does not—establish.

Evidence, not a security certificate

Exact canary matching can miss paraphrased leaks. Retrieval checks depend on complete, truthful instrumentation. An application returning nothing can pass containment; permitted retrieval is reported separately and does not establish answer quality.

Synthetic examples, not real-model findings

The sample report uses mocks. The SQLite exhibit demonstration follows one scripted instruction, not an LLM's decision. Real-model experiments and independent validation remain future work.

Built for developers and technical reviewers

Use the evidence to investigate authorization boundaries in isolated test applications. Use invented records only. This page collects no inputs and contains no scripts or analytics.
